# Corkcrete: Cork-Hemp-Lime Insulation for BioDomes

A recommended report on using cork-hemp-lime composite as wall insulation in BioDome construction, with emphasis on Portuguese material availability.

---

## What Is Corkcrete?

Corkcrete is a bio-composite insulation made from **expanded cork granules** and **hemp hurd** bound with **natural hydraulic lime (NHL)**. It combines cork's exceptional moisture resistance with hemp's structural fiber in a breathable, fire-resistant matrix.

The result is a high-performance natural insulation that can be:

- **Packed into wall cavities** as primary infill between structural poles
- **Applied as an insulation layer** on interior or exterior wall faces
- **Used in roof assemblies** under or above living roof substrates

## Why Cork + Hemp?

Neither material alone is ideal. Together they compensate for each other's weaknesses:

| Property | Cork Alone | Hemp Alone | Cork + Hemp |
|----------|-----------|------------|-------------|
| Thermal insulation | Excellent | Good | Excellent |
| Moisture resistance | Excellent (suberin) | Moderate (absorbs water) | Very good |
| Structural cohesion | Poor (crumbly) | Good (fibrous) | Good |
| Binder adhesion | Moderate | Excellent | Good |
| Drying time | Fast | Slow | Moderate |
| Cost (in Portugal) | Low | Moderate | Low-moderate |

Cork granules provide the insulation and moisture resistance. Hemp hurd provides the fibrous matrix that holds the mix together and bonds well with lime. The lime binder provides fire resistance, alkalinity (inhibiting mold), and long-term carbonation strength.

## Material Properties

| Property | Cork Granules | Hemp Hurd | Corkcrete (blended) |
|----------|--------------|-----------|---------------------|
| Bulk density (kg/m³) | 65-80 | 100-120 | 280-350 |
| Thermal conductivity (W/mK) | 0.037-0.040 | 0.048-0.060 | 0.065-0.080 |
| R-value per 100mm | ~2.6 | ~1.8 | ~1.4-1.5 |
| Vapor permeability | High | High | High |
| Fire class | E (self-extinguishing) | E | B1 (with lime binder) |
| Water absorption | Very low | Moderate | Low-moderate |

> **Cork quality matters.** Use **expanded cork granules** (3-10mm), not raw cork dust. Expanded granules have been heat-treated, which activates the natural suberin resin and improves dimensional stability. Raw cork dust absorbs too much water from the lime mix and compromises performance.

## Recommended Mix Ratios

Three proven ratios suited to different applications:

| Mix | Cork : Hemp : Lime : Water | Density (kg/m³) | Best For |
|-----|---------------------------|-----------------|----------|
| **Insulation** | 3 : 1 : 1.5 : 1.5 | ~280 | Maximum thermal performance, interior layer |
| **Balanced** | 2 : 2 : 1.5 : 1.5 | ~320 | General-purpose wall infill |
| **Structural** | 1 : 3 : 2 : 1.5 | ~350 | Load-bearing partitions, exterior layer |

All ratios are by **volume**. Lime is **NHL 3.5** (natural hydraulic lime).

> **Do not substitute Portland cement.** Cement creates a rigid, vapor-closed matrix that traps moisture and cracks against a natural timber frame. NHL lime remains flexible, breathable, and self-healing through carbonation.

## Mixing Process

**Equipment:** Mortar mixer or large tub, shovel, spray bottle, bucket measures.

1. **Measure dry aggregates.** Combine cork granules and hemp hurd in the mixer or tub. Toss by hand until evenly distributed.

2. **Add lime.** Sprinkle NHL 3.5 over the aggregate and mix dry for 1-2 minutes until the powder coats the particles evenly. Both cork and hemp should look dusty grey.

3. **Add water gradually.** Pour half the water in and mix. Add the rest slowly while mixing. The target consistency is damp but not wet — squeeze a handful and it should hold its shape without dripping.

4. **Rest 5 minutes.** Let the lime begin to hydrate. Mix again briefly before use.

### The Squeeze Test

Grab a fistful of the mix and squeeze firmly. It should:

- **Hold together** when you open your hand
- **Not drip** water
- **Crumble slightly** at the edges

If it falls apart, add water in small splashes. If it drips, add more dry aggregate.

## Application Methods

### Wall Cavity Infill

Pack the **Balanced** or **Structural** mix directly between structural poles as the primary wall material.

1. Pack corkcrete between poles in **15-20cm lifts**
2. Tamp firmly with a flat board — the wall should feel springy, not solid
3. Score the top of each lift before adding the next for better adhesion
4. Target final wall thickness: **30-40cm**
5. Allow **3-5 weeks** to cure depending on humidity and thickness
6. Apply lime render to both interior and exterior faces

### Interior Insulation Layer

Apply a 75-100mm corkcrete layer on the interior face of an existing wall.

1. Ensure the existing wall surface is clean and dry
2. Mist the wall surface lightly to improve adhesion
3. Apply the **Insulation** mix in 40-50mm lifts, tamping lightly
4. Score the surface of the first lift before applying the second
5. Allow **2-3 weeks** to cure before applying lime render

### Exterior Insulation

For adding insulation to the outside of an existing structure:

1. Attach a light wattle or mesh framework to the exterior wall face
2. Pack the **Insulation** mix into the framework in 40-50mm lifts
3. Total thickness: **50-75mm**
4. Protect from rain during the 3-week curing period
5. Apply exterior lime render once cured

## Thermal Performance

A typical BioDome wall assembly using corkcrete:

| Layer (inside to outside) | Thickness | R-value |
|---------------------------|-----------|---------|
| Interior lime render | 15mm | 0.02 |
| Corkcrete insulation layer | 100mm | 1.5 |
| Corkcrete wall infill (Balanced) | 300mm | 4.3 |
| Exterior lime render | 20mm | 0.02 |
| **Total** | **435mm** | **~5.8** |

For comparison, a 100mm corkcrete insulation layer alone adds R-1.5 — a meaningful upgrade for winter comfort in northern Portugal where temperatures drop to 2-5°C.

## Moisture Behavior

Cork's natural **suberin** content gives corkcrete a significant moisture advantage:

- **Capillary absorption** is 40-60% lower than hemp-only (hempcrete) mixes
- **Drying rate** is faster — cork doesn't hold water the way hemp hurd does
- **No vapor barrier needed** — the entire wall assembly remains breathable
- **Mold resistance** — the alkaline lime environment plus cork's suberin inhibit fungal growth
- **Seasonal buffering** — the wall absorbs humidity in summer and releases it in winter, stabilizing indoor relative humidity

These properties make corkcrete particularly well-suited to Portugal's Atlantic climate, where winter damp is the primary threat to natural buildings.

## Fire Safety

- The **lime binder** is non-combustible and provides the primary fire resistance
- **Cork** chars on the surface but is naturally self-extinguishing — suberin doesn't sustain flame
- **Hemp hurd** is protected by the lime coating
- The blended composite achieves **Class B1** fire rating when lime content is adequate
- Corkcrete walls with lime render on both faces provide 60-90 minutes of fire resistance

## Recommendation

Corkcrete is the recommended insulation system for BioDomes built in Portugal. It offers the best balance of:

- **Thermal performance** — significantly better than hemp-only mixes
- **Moisture resilience** — critical for Atlantic climates
- **Material availability** — cork is local and inexpensive in Portugal
- **Frame compatibility** — flexible enough to accommodate timber movement
- **Sustainability** — carbon-negative, fully biodegradable, no petrochemicals
- **Cost** — modest addition to overall build budget

The **Balanced mix (2:2:1.5:1.5)** is recommended as the default for most BioDome wall applications. Use the **Insulation mix** for dedicated insulation layers where maximum thermal performance is needed, and the **Structural mix** for partitions or areas requiring greater compressive strength.
