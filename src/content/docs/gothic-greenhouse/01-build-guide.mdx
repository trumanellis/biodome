A 3m × 10m gothic-arch tunnel greenhouse with a 6.5m pointed apex — tall enough for citrus, banana, papaya, or other small fruit trees grown indoors. The frame is built from 6 pairs of long eucalyptus poles, each pair meeting at a sharp gothic point. Two off-the-roll 8m × 10m plastic sheets cover the structure — one on each slope — meeting at the apex with a 30cm continuous ridge vent that runs the full 10m length. The ridge vent provides a high-level outlet for warm air; summer performance also depends on inlet area, weather and shading.

:::caution[Design Status — Resolve Before Procurement or Excavation]
This is a coordinated design draft, not a completed construction specification. The chosen arrangement is a wide level terrace, east–west ridge, six arches, two above-ground gabions and twelve permanent pipe sleeves, with no geotextile included in the proposed arch-foot assembly.

Before buying pipe, harvesting a full set of poles or digging, resolve the minimum timber sections and connections; sleeve material, diameter, wall thickness, support, drainage and removable restraint; the lifting and temporary-bracing sequence; and the plastic fixing, roll-up and vent-control details. Confirm that the soil/backfill detail works without fabric. The 40cm hole depth and ~35cm pole-seat depth are provisional layout dimensions, not verified foundation capacities. Rendered pipe and pole sizes are illustrative.
:::

:::tip[Designed Around Two 8m × 10m Plastic Sheets]
The target is to cover each slope with one existing 8m × 10m sheet. Confirm the fit before fixing the frame dimensions:

- **8m direction** = drape distance per side from the gabion cap (~45cm above terrace grade) to its ridge pole. Each arch pole is provisionally **about 8.4m long**, including ~35cm below grade on a bearing stone in a 40cm-deep posthole. The pole slides down a permanent sleeve extending through the cage and into the hole. Check the actual curve with a rope before cutting the plastic, allowing for the ridge fold and roll-up batten.
- **10m direction** = tunnel length, gable to gable.

The two sheets meet at the apex but don't touch — they're each lashed to one of two parallel ridge poles, leaving a continuous ~30cm gap. A separate vent cap drops over the gap to seal it in cold or wet weather; open it using the terrace-accessible operating arrangement described in the covering section.

Measure the selected arch profile from the 45cm cap to its ridge pole with a rope, then add the ridge fold and base-batten allowance. Measure end fixings too: a 10m-long sheet over a 10m pole grid has no spare length for gable wraps. Resolve those fixings or adjust the covered length before erection. Pole length, curved drape length and sheet width are different measurements; an approximate straight-line formula does not establish the fit.
:::

## How It Works

The frame is built from 6 gothic arches, each made of two long eucalyptus poles meeting at a sharp point. Two continuous **gabion cages** run beneath the pole rows, 3m apart. Every arch pole passes down through its cage into a **permanent pipe sleeve** extending through the cage into its individual posthole. The sleeve stays in place when the pole is inserted or replaced. The current layout shows a pole butt about **35cm below terrace grade** in a 40cm-deep hole; final sleeve and footing dimensions require checking. The cage and posthole are two parts of the same foot, not alternatives. Six arches stand 2m on-centre over the 10m length, meeting at a 6.5m apex. The chosen site is now a **wide, flat terrace in central Portugal**. The ridge runs **east–west**, so the two long walls face **south and north**. Both gables and both long sides have level working space. Keep the shorter dry-stone terrace wall at least **1m beyond the outside face of the nearest gabion**, increasing that clearance wherever drainage or access equipment needs it.

Two parallel ridge poles run the full length of the tunnel, lashed to opposite faces of each apex crossing. The ~30cm gap between them is the **ridge vent** — covered by a hinged plastic cap lashed to one ridge pole, with a terrace-accessible control and restraint arrangement for opening and closing. Six longitudinal purlins — three per side — span between the arches at hip, shoulder, and below-apex heights, supporting the plastic and locking the arches together.

The two main plastic sheets each cover one slope: 8m runs from the plinth cap up and over its own ridge pole; 10m runs along the tunnel length. Battens lashed to the purlins sandwich the plastic without punctures. Gable ends are framed with diagonal infill poles and clad in any spare plastic, shade cloth, or reused tarp.

**Why so tall:**
- **Fits citrus and small fruit trees.** A semi-dwarf grapefruit (3–4.5m) clears the centre easily, with growing room. A standard kumquat or Meyer lemon disappears into the volume.
- **Stack effect ventilation.** The 6.5m apex with a continuous ridge vent creates a strong stack — hot air rises and exits along the full 10m, pulling cool air in through the doors and lower walls. No fans needed.
- **Light from above.** With a tall narrow profile, sunlight hits leaves from a much higher angle through the day — better for tropical-feeling plants that want overhead light.

**What you need:**
- **Site:** A wide, flat terrace with space for the 3m × 10m pole grid, the 40cm-wide cages, clear access around both sides and gables, drainage, and material staging. Set the nearest outer gabion face at least 1m back from the shorter dry-stone terrace wall; confirm the actual usable width before setting out.
- **People:** Crew size and lifting equipment to be established from measured pole weights and the checked erection method; close-up photos do not show a complete lifting crew.
- **Tools:** Drawknife or machete, shovel, wire cutters and fencing pliers (or a gabion lacing tool), tape measure, spirit level, a long straight edge, scissors or utility knife, suitable guarded access equipment selected for the actual working heights, reach, terrace bearing, and available working width.
- **Time:** ~1 week of pole prep (Regenerative Default) or 2 weeks (Extended-Life Protocol with borax soak), then foundation, frame and covering phases scheduled after design and equipment selection. Include the specified foundation inspection/settlement interval; gathering and hauling stone are additional.

## Before Procurement and Site Work

### Minimum-Weight Eucalyptus — Sizing Brief

The objective is **the thinnest, lightest timber that passes the checks**, including handling during erection. No safe minimum diameter has yet been established for this 6.5m-tall, six-arch frame on the wide terrace. The former 9–11cm recommendation was not a verified design either; removing it does not approve smaller stock.

**Why diameter matters:** for an ideal solid round section of the same material and length, mass varies with diameter squared, bending section modulus with diameter cubed, and bending stiffness with diameter to the fourth power. These comparisons isolate geometry; they are not capacities for tapered eucalyptus poles.

| Example section diameter | Relative mass | Relative bending section modulus | Relative bending stiffness |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10cm reference | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| 8cm comparison | 64% | 51% | 41% |
| 7cm comparison | 49% | 34% | 24% |
| 6cm comparison | 36% | 22% | 13% |

These are **comparison cases, not an approved harvesting schedule**. Equations and roundwood design considerations are described by the [University of Alaska’s round-timber guidance](https://www.uaf.edu/ces/publications/database/housing/allowable-loads.php) and the [USDA Wood Handbook](https://research.fs.usda.gov/fpl/wood-handbook).

Before choosing stock:

- Measure each candidate’s debarked butt diameter, diameter at every purlin/lashing station, and small-end diameter. Record species, knots, cracks, sweep, grain deviations, and moisture condition. Butt diameter alone does not describe the weakest section.
- Weigh representative full-length green poles. Use those measured masses to set the lifting plan; an ideal-cylinder estimate is a poor substitute for a tapered, wet 8.4m pole.
- Have the complete frame checked for local wind, relevant snow/rain loading, bending, compression/buckling, deflection, green-wood creep, lashings, splices, bracing, and foundation restraint. Include erection stages, local exposure, and the actual terrace ground conditions. Determine minimum sections at the critical stations, not just a nominal butt size.
- Assess arches, ridges, purlins, and gable members separately. Use lighter infill where its span and load permit. Shorter lifting pieces or closer support spacing may help, but changing splices, arch count, or bracing requires a coordinated revision rather than a diameter swap.

The renders illustrate geometry and construction sequence; their apparent timber thickness is not a sizing specification. Keep the existing six-arch layout and buried-foot details as the basis for the check. If that geometry cannot meet the handling goal with sound thin stock, revisit height, spacing, or structural system before harvesting the full set.

### Working Access on the Wide Terrace

Use the **level terrace on both sides** for installation and maintenance. Plan exterior working positions along the north and south walls, with access around both gables. The 6.5m ridge and 4.5m upper purlins still need suitable working equipment.

Use an interior platform for suitable ridge tasks and guarded exterior access on the level terrace for battens, gable corners and other outside fixings. Select equipment for the task, height, reach, support and available width. **A 1m cage-to-wall gap is not automatically enough for a tower, its stabilisers or its repositioning route.** If the equipment will not fit, move the greenhouse farther inward before digging. Do not stand equipment on the dry-stone wall cap, brace it against the unverified eucalyptus frame, or use loose blocks to level it.

The ridge task is **6.5m above the working terrace**. Sustained two-handed sheet handling and lashing should be planned from suitable work platforms; a short stepladder is not the default. Confirm firm bearing and protect people from the remaining terrace boundary where needed. See [access selection](https://www.hse.gov.uk/work-at-height/step-by-step-guide.htm) and [scaffold support guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/scaffoldinginfo.htm) for general principles, rather than local regulatory requirements.

### Prepare the Timber

The arch pole feet are **below grade inside permanent sleeves**. Debark, trial the curve without cutting fibres, and protect the below-grade butt, groundline band and sleeve-enclosed timber using the [Treatment Guide](/fundamentals/03-treatment/). Outside fire restrictions, use its charring protocol; when open flame is prohibited, use its [no-flame membrane-boot protocol](/fundamentals/03-treatment/#tier-1--the-membrane-boot-36pole--the-workhorse), including the specified drying and fitting sequence. Confirm that the selected treatment fits the sleeve clearance, drainage and removable restraint; a sleeve does not by itself protect timber from decay. Seal the timber after drying and maintain drainage.

The four gable door posts also use treated feet in separate gravel postholes. An optional borax treatment remains available through the [Extended-Life Protocol](/fundamentals/03-treatment/#protocol-b--the-extended-life-protocol).

<Steps>

1. **Harvest poles**

   Choose the **lightest sound eucalyptus sections that satisfy the frame and connection checks**, rather than selecting the largest trunks or a fixed tree age. The earlier 9–11cm arch-butt range was an unverified allowance, not a demonstrated minimum. Species, taper, defects, moisture, wind exposure, bracing, and connection details must be considered together. See the sizing brief above before harvesting a full set.

   - **Arch poles:** 14 (12 + 2 spares), minimum diameter and taper to be established by the frame check, ~8.4m length (including ~35cm below grade; check actual curve and apex overlap), *E. globulus* — select sound, suitably tapered poles; weigh representative stock before committing.
   - **Ridge poles:** 4 (two per side, overlapped), diameter to be checked for the 2m arch bays and splice/lashing details, ~5.5m length each.
   - **Purlin poles:** 12 (two per run × 6 runs), diameter to be checked for the 2m bays, plastic loads, and connections, ~5.5m length each.
   - **Gable poles:** supplier/cutting schedule, diameter to suit each member’s checked span and load; allow up to ~4.5m for the central infill (gables are big triangles — you'll need a few long pieces).
   - **Door posts:** 4 (2 per gable), diameter to be checked with the gable frame, ~2.4m length.

   :::caution[Keep Structural Poles Intact]
   Do not aggressively score or notch the arch poles to force the curve. Cutting fibres changes the structural section and creates local weaknesses, especially in thinner material. Select naturally suitable green poles and trial the required curve with controlled support; reject splitting, sharp kinks, or damaged stock. Any kerfing, lamination, or bundled-pole alternative needs its own connection and member design.
   :::

2. **Debark within 48 hours.** Bark left on traps moisture and accelerates rot. Strip with a drawknife or flat-blade scraper, butt to thin end.

3. **Trial the curve and confirm sleeve fit.** Check representative green stock against the full-size profile using controlled supports. Confirm the treated butt can enter the chosen sleeve and reach its support without binding. Record taper, bending behaviour and mass; a successful bend does not establish structural capacity.

4. **Carry out the chosen treatment protocol.** Follow its preparation, optional borax, drying and buried-foot protection order as one sequence. Treat arch feet and the four door-post feet; confirm sleeve compatibility before treating the full batch.

5. **Sort and pair.** Sort all 14 arch poles by butt diameter. Pair them so each arch has two poles of similar size (6 working pairs + 1 spare pair). Both sleeve supports in each pair are set to the same checked level below terrace grade — one in the left row, one in the right — so taper matching across the apex matters for a balanced apex crossing.

6. **Release the prepared stock for erection.** Complete the selected treatment and drying sequence, label the pairs and keep the matched poles supported until the foundation and lifting arrangements are ready.

</Steps>

:::tip[Linseed Oil Sealing — Don't Skip]
Whichever protocol you choose, seal all above-ground pole surfaces with hot linseed oil (60–70°C, brushed on) once the frame is up and the structure has dried for 2–3 weeks. The linseed step is doing real work — it's the *outside* preservative complement to whatever you've put inside the wood. **Reapply every 5–10 years.** This is the single most important maintenance task for the Regenerative Default protocol.
:::

## Phase 1 — Postholes, Permanent Sleeves and Above-Ground Gabions

This guide uses **two long stone-filled wire cages**, one along each side. Each is about **40cm wide × 45cm high**, sitting entirely above prepared terrace ground. The pole grid is 3m × 10m; extend each cage about 20cm beyond each end pole so the end pockets are enclosed, giving a physical wall length of about **10.4m**. Keep both gable doorways open.

Each plinth is a continuous assembly of joined, internally braced basket sections, not one unsupported length of mesh. Its level cap supports the roll-up batten directly, so there is **no separate rail pole**. The twelve arch pole butts sit about 35cm below terrace grade inside permanent pipe sleeves extending through the cage bottoms into the individual postholes. Only the twelve postholes are excavated; the cages have no buried portion. Allow about **3.75m³ of cage fill before deducting pole pockets** (2 × 10.4 × 0.4 × 0.45); obtain a packing and hauling allowance from the supplier.

### Wide Terrace Layout and South-Facing Long Wall

Build on the **wide, flat terrace on terraced land in central Portugal**, with level ground beside both long walls and beyond both gables. Set out the greenhouse, drainage, material staging and working routes together before excavating.

- **Orientation:** Set the 10m ridge **east–west**, giving a **south-facing long wall** and a north-facing long wall. The drawings use the west gable as the main entrance, so south is on the right when looking in through that entrance. The east gable also opens onto terrace ground.
- **Boundary clearance:** Leave **at least 1m horizontally from the outer gabion face to the nearer edge/top of the shorter dry-stone terrace wall**. This is a minimum layout allowance, not a calculated safe setback or a platform-width specification. Leave more space wherever the existing wall, ground, drainage, or selected equipment needs it. Do not use the wall cap as working space or foundation support.
- **Working area:** Reserve firm, level access along both 10m walls and around both gables. Measure platform feet/outriggers and the access route before committing to the footprint; keep drains, stored stone and plants out of that area. The drawings show the low boundary south of the greenhouse for clarity; confirm its actual position on site.
- **Sun and ventilation:** Check winter shading from trees, higher terraces and nearby structures, plus summer ventilation and shading needs. East–west is the selected site orientation, not a claim that it is optimal for every crop or wind direction. [Greenhouse orientation guidance](https://ceac.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/G%20Glazing.pdf) distinguishes winter light from light uniformity through the year.
- **Drainage and support:** Keep the cage bases and all postholes drained to suitable outlets. Collect roof runoff or use shallow drainage where it will not saturate the working strip, erode the terrace edge, or load the retaining wall with water. The shorter wall's height and construction are not surveyed here; check its condition and the bearing of the retained ground before excavation, stockpiling, or placing access equipment nearby.

Use galvanized or suitably coated gabion mesh with openings compatible with the fill. For 10cm openings, select durable stones larger than 10cm for the faces; do not rely on loose undersized rubble that can escape. Buy or fabricate connected sections to suit the specified 40cm width and 45cm height, with diaphragms, lacing, and bracing matched to the system. Five nominal 2m sections plus end extensions per side are one possible layout; the pole pockets must remain accessible at all six stations.

## Phase 2 — Frame, Twin Ridges, Purlins and Gables

The arch poles are provisionally about 8.4m long and reach a 6.5m apex. Arrange the checked lifting equipment, crew, temporary supports and high-level access before starting. A fixed sleeve changes how the butt can be inserted; the old ground-pivot walk-up method is not the erection method for this assembly.

### Raising the Tall Gothic Arches

Lay out and pair the poles in the clear staging area on the terrace. Start at the west gable and work east, keeping both side routes and the east gable clear for the crew and equipment. Set temporary props on firm terrace ground, outside the walking routes; keep their supports and all equipment clear of the dry-stone boundary wall. Move high-level access along the level terrace as the frame advances.

Each arch is two poles. Each pole slides into the permanent sleeve already installed through the cage, to its specified support about 35cm below terrace grade; its thin end rises ~1m vertically, then bends sharply inward and upward, meeting its partner at the apex 6.5m above the ground. The two poles cross and overlap by 30–40cm at the apex.

<Steps>

1. **First arch (one gable end)**

   Use the erection method developed for the selected pole weight and sleeve clearance. It must establish how each butt is lifted, aligned and lowered into its fixed sleeve without using the pipe rim as a pivot or damaging it. Do not assume a long pole can be seated while horizontal and then walked upright inside the pipe.

   Seat each pole on its specified support. Fit its erection-stage restraint and temporary bracing before the lifting support is released; retain the movement needed for the checked arch-forming sequence. Bring the paired tops to the required crossing using the planned supports and high-level access, then install the specified apex connection. Keep the first arch supported until the later bracing release check.

2. **Second arch (next station)**

   Move 2m down the tunnel and raise Pair 2 the same way. Now you have two parallel gothic arches, 2m apart. Install and retain the specified restraints and temporary bracing for this stage; the ridges alone are not a bracing release criterion.

3. **Set the first pair of ridge poles**

   This design uses **two parallel ridge poles**, ~30cm apart, one for each side of the apex. The gap between them becomes the continuous ridge vent.

   Lift the first ridge pole (about 5.5m long, with its diameter established by the frame check) up onto the **north side (left when looking east from the west entrance)** of the apex crossings of Arches 1 and 2. Position it to the checked apex detail so that the clear gap between the two ridge attachments is about 30cm overall. Lash it to each apex crossing with galvanized wire.

   Lift the second ridge pole the same way, but onto the **south side (right from that entrance)**. The two ridge poles are now parallel along the apex, separated by the ~30cm ridge-vent gap across the tunnel.

   Lash both poles tightly. Retain the temporary bracing until the complete frame, permanent bracing, base restraints and connections have passed the specified release check.

4. **Remaining four arches**

   Raise Arches 3, 4, 5, and 6 at 2m spacing along the rest of the tunnel. After each new arch, lash both apex crossings to both ridge poles. Two 5.5m pieces with a 0.5m overlap give 10.5m before trimming. The first piece from station 0m ends at 5.5m, before Arch 4 at 6m. Arrange the checked splice and temporary support before raising beyond that reach; a splice between arches needs its own connection detail. Continue to Arch 6 without loading an unsupported ridge end.

5. **Trim and cap the ridges**

   Trim both ridge poles flush with the gable ends. Both ridges should now span the full 10m length, locked to all 6 arch crossings.

6. **Inspect the previously installed pole restraints**

   Inspect and complete the specified permanent restraints installed progressively as each pole was seated; do not defer all base restraint until the six arches are standing. Do not pour gravel, cage stone or concrete between the pole and pipe. Keep the sleeve drainage clear and protect the timber using the treatment protocol. The restraint and sleeve-to-foundation connection must be resolved before erection.

   <figure>
     <img src="/assets/fig-gothic-buried-cage-section.svg" alt="Section showing a eucalyptus pole inside a permanent sleeve extending through an above-ground gabion into a separate posthole" />
     <figcaption>The pipe stays in place. Stone surrounds the outside of the sleeve; the pole remains removable. Drainage and restraint details require specification.</figcaption>
   </figure>

7. **Close the access lids and level the caps**

   Close and lace the lid panels around each permanent sleeve, leaving a removable access section for future repairs. Finish the cap with stable, flat stones and keep cut wire ends away from the plastic. The roll-up batten will rest directly on the apron over this cap; no rail poles are needed.

</Steps>

:::caution[Don't Skip the Twin Ridges]
A single ridge pole down the centre of a tunnel this tall would force you to seal the apex with tape — and a sealed 6.5m apex turns into a chimney that boils plants on hot days. The twin ridges carry the two sheet edges and define the vent opening. They do not replace the permanent bracing or establish frame stability by themselves.
:::

### Longitudinal Purlins

Three purlins per side, lashed to the outside of each arch — one at hip height (~1m), one at shoulder height (~2m), and one near apex height (~4.5m). For a tunnel this tall the upper purlin is genuinely high; use the planned access equipment for the upper joints, on both sides of the frame.

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/fig-gothic-purlins.svg" alt="Three-quarter view showing six longitudinal purlins on the tall tunnel — three per side at hip, shoulder, and below-apex heights" />
  <figcaption>Purlin layout: three poles per side at roughly 1m, 2m, and 4.5m heights, lashed to the outside face of each arch where they cross</figcaption>
</figure>

<Steps>

1. **Prepare the checked purlin stock**

   Use the 12 purlin poles from the prepared cutting schedule — two per run, six runs. Confirm their condition and treatment before installation. Each run spans the full 10m length as two ~5.5m poles with a checked 50cm overlap, trimmed from 10.5m to 10m.

2. **Install purlins, lowest first**

   Starting on one side at hip height (~1m above ground), hold a purlin pole horizontally against the outside face of all 6 arches and lash with galvanized wire at each crossing. Where the purlin runs out, use the checked 50cm splice, support it as specified and trim the completed run to length.

3. **Repeat at shoulder and near-apex heights**

   Install the second purlin at ~2m and the third at ~4.5m (under the apex curve). Mirror everything on the other side.

4. **Inspect the frame before releasing temporary supports**

   Check geometry, splices, lashings, sleeve restraints and the specified permanent bracing against the completed design. A hand-push test is not a capacity check. Retain temporary supports until the release conditions in the erection plan are met. See [temporary-support planning principles](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/temporary-works.htm); this reference is not a statement of Portuguese regulatory requirements.

</Steps>

### Gable End Framing

Gables for a 6.5m-tall tunnel are large triangles — about 14m² each. You can't span them with a few diagonals; you need a small framework.

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/fig-gothic-plinth-gable.svg" alt="Front elevation of one gable end showing the tall gothic arch outline, door frame, and infill poles dividing the triangle into manageable bays" />
  <figcaption>Gable framing: the outermost arch defines the gothic profile; door frame plus the checked vertical and side infill members subdivide the gable for cladding</figcaption>
</figure>

<Steps>

1. **Connect the prepared door frames**

   Door posts are already standing, with their lintel at ~2.1m. Confirm plumb, then connect each frame to the gable using the specified infill connections. The 2.1m door posts do not directly cross the tall outer arch.

2. **Vertical infill above the door**

   Cut a vertical pole (diameter from the gable check, 4–4.5m long) and lash it from the centre of the door lintel up to the apex. This divides the gable above the door into two narrow bays — much easier to clad.

3. **Diagonal infill on each side**

   Cut the two side infill poles per gable to the checked gable layout. Connect each door-frame side to the corresponding outer arch at the specified attachment points; measure these members on the erected frame before cutting. This divides each side of the gable into two bays.

4. **Repeat at the other gable and release the completed frame.**

   Complete the permanent bracing and frame inspection before removing temporary supports or fitting plastic. Keep planned access equipment in place for the covering phase.

</Steps>

  <img src="/assets/gothic-wide-terrace-bare-frame.png" alt="Six gothic arches on two gabion cages, with flat working ground all around" />
  <figcaption>Six arches and five bays on the wide terrace. Pole feet continue through the cages into the buried postholes; rendered timber thickness is illustrative.</figcaption>

## Phase 3 — Plastic, Ridge Vent and Commissioning

### Materials

- **Two main plastic sheets:** each 8m × 10m, 150μm UV-stabilised polyethylene. The whole tunnel is sized around two such sheets — one per slope.
- **Vent cap sheet:** 1m × 11m strip (cut from offcuts or a third small sheet). This is the hinged cap that covers the ridge vent gap.
- **Gable cladding:** ~28m² total (two ~14m² triangles, with margin). UV PE, shade cloth, or scrap tarp.
- **Greenhouse repair tape:** 2 rolls.
- **Twine or poly rope:** ~150m.
- **Split-pole battens:** ~30 pieces, 2–3cm thick × 1–1.5m long.

### Prepare Before Covering

1. Measure the exterior working areas, equipment footprint and routes around both gables. Arrange the platforms and any boundary protection before handling sheets. Keep material stockpiles clear of the wall and working strips.
2. Choose a calm weather window. Use a clean staging area on the wide terrace to measure, mark and fold both 8m × 10m sheets for controlled feeding. Prepare smooth battens, lashings and base assemblies at ground level.
3. While the frame is bare, fit and test the apron attachments, completed drainage routes and sheet-fixing hardware on both sides. Keep the working strip usable and the sheet edges clear of sharp mesh and stone.
4. Fit and trial the selected roll-up and ridge-vent controls while the fixings are accessible. Confirm the roll-up travel against purlin fixings: permanently clamping a sheet at the 1m purlin prevents rolling it above that line. Resolve releasable fixings or another checked panel arrangement before cladding; extra pieces must be added to the cutting schedule. Controls should be operable from terrace level or a gable, without routine climbing.
5. Agree the sheet-feeding method between the terrace crew, interior platform and exterior work positions. Keep the access equipment until both sheets, all exterior battens, drainage and ventilation restraints have been inspected.

### Covering the Tunnel

<Steps>

1. **Identify the south and north sides**

   Label the two sheets **south-facing slope** and **north-facing slope**. With the main entrance at the west gable, south is on the right and north on the left when looking in. Each takes one 8m × 10m sheet. Complete the working-access plan before starting.

2. **Feed and secure the south-facing sheet**

   While the frame is open, bring the prepared south sheet from the terrace staging area to the agreed feeding position. Crews at the ridge access and south-side work platform feed and control it together. Use the clear terrace strip beside the south wall to stage the folded sheet and move the exterior platform along the bays. Keep the north-side route and both gables open; do not leave a large sheet loose in the wind.

   From suitable ridge access, fold the top edge over the south ridge pole with the specified overlap and secure it at the marked stations. From the south exterior work position, guide the sheet down to the cap, temporarily restrain it, and leave the allowance for the base batten. Check alignment before fully securing it.


3. **Fit the north-facing sheet**

   Move the staging and exterior working position to the north side, using the clear route around a gable. Fit the north sheet to the north ridge pole using the planned north-side and interior access. Keep the two sheets separate at the apex, leaving the continuous ~30cm vent gap. Maintain safe access and exit routes while completing both sides.

4. **Lash to purlins (sandwich method)**

   Fit the exterior battens at the fixed-sheet purlin lines in the resolved cladding layout. Keep the intended roll-up travel free of permanent clamps; use only the specified releasable or separate-panel detail there. The [batten sandwich illustration](/greenhouse/01-build-guide/#covering-the-dome) shows a principle, not a complete fixing or roll-up specification. Work from suitable exterior platforms positioned on the level terrace on each side. The outside battens still need outside access; rolling a sheet down from inside does not make these fixings reachable through the plastic.

5. **Fit the drip aprons**

   With access still in place, finish the apron connections on both sides before securing the main sheets at the base. Keep the main sheet lower edges temporarily restrained but liftable for the overlap. Fit clear UV-resistant strips about 1.5m wide × 10.5m long, adjusted to the drainage routes. Secure them at smooth cap attachments beneath the roll-up battens and lap the wall sheets over them. Carry runoff into the selected shallow drains or collection channels without saturating the working strips or discharging onto the dry-stone wall. Keep the minimum cage-to-wall gap clear, enlarging the layout if the apron and equipment need more space.

   <figure>
     <img src="/assets/gothic-plinth-drip-apron.png" alt="Rain running down the clear greenhouse skin onto an apron over a continuous gabion wall and outward to a shallow swale" />
     <figcaption>Apron detail for the wide terrace. Use a suitable drain or collection channel on each side, while preserving working access and keeping runoff away from the dry-stone boundary wall.</figcaption>
   </figure>

6. **Anchor the long base edges on the plinth caps**

   Fit each full-length base batten from the terrace or suitable platform, with the wall sheet lapping over the apron and the batten restrained against wind. Connect the chosen roll-up controls and test opening, closing and restraint from accessible terrace-level positions. Keep the operation clear of the low boundary wall.

7. **Install the ridge vent cap**

   From suitable ridge access, lash the 1m × 11m vent cap to the north ridge pole over the north sheet’s fold, with its loose edge lapping onto the south sheet. Finish the south-side edge and restraints from the planned exterior access. Keep the slot clear of operating hardware.

   Provide the specified overlap, approximately 20cm, onto the opposite sheet. The closing and restraint detail must be compatible with the selected operating arrangement and resist wind; do not leave loose stones where they could fall onto workers or lower ground.

   Test the selected vent control from its terrace-accessible position. Loose prop sticks that require someone to climb to the apex each day are not the operating method for this site. The ventilation diagram shows the airflow principle; it does not specify the access or operating hardware.

   <figure>
     <img src="/assets/fig-gothic-ventilation.svg" alt="Side view showing the ridge vent in operation — vent cap open, with operating hardware omitted, hot air rising and exiting through the ridge gap, cool air entering through doors and roll-up walls" />
     <figcaption>Ridge vent operation: cap propped open creates a continuous 10m slot vent. Hot air rises and exits through the gap; cool air is drawn in low through the doors and roll-up walls. This diagram shows airflow only; the operating hardware requires a separate detail.</figcaption>
   </figure>

8. **Cover the gables**

   Work from the level terrace at each gable using suitable high-level access. Reach the corner fixings from the adjacent side platform rather than overreaching. Cut the gable cladding with 20cm margin, reinforce the joints with greenhouse repair tape, and trim the bottom edge to the intended ground/cap line.

9. **Fit the door curtains to the existing frames**

   At each door opening, fold the cut plastic edges over and tape to themselves for reinforcement. Make a door flap 20cm wider than the opening and 20cm taller. Tape or lash across the top of the door frame so it hangs as a curtain. Weight the bottom edge with a thin pole sewn into a fold.

10. **Commission before removing the working access**

    Check all fixed edges, roll-up travel, gable closures and wind restraints. Operate both roll-up sides and the ridge cap from their intended ground-level positions. Test water flow through aprons, drains and sleeve outlets; correct pooling or leakage. Inspect the frame and foundation connections, clear working paths and record the maintenance schedule before putting the greenhouse into use.

</Steps>

### Ventilation Modes

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/gothic-wide-terrace-side-ventilation.png" alt="South-facing long wall with roll-up ventilation and a clear level strip before a low dry-stone boundary" />
  <figcaption>South elevation: the low boundary is separate from the gabion, leaving a level route for installation, ventilation and maintenance.</figcaption>
</figure>

The ridge vent + roll-up walls + gable doors give you three distinct climate modes:

- **Sealed (winter):** Vent cap closed and restrained using its selected controls. Roll-up walls down. Doors closed. Tunnel acts as a passive solar greenhouse — captures and holds heat.
- **Mild (spring/autumn):** Vent opened using the accessible operating arrangement. Roll-up walls opened using their accessible controls to the first purlin (~1m). One or both doors open. Strong stack effect pulls cool air in low and pushes hot air out high.
- **Hot (peak summer):** Vent cap fully open. Roll-up walls raised using their accessible controls on both sides (you'll have a near-open structure at ground level). Both doors open. The clear roof remains overhead; it does not provide shade. Fit a separately specified shade system if needed.

## Maintenance

**Weekly during summer:** Check the ridge vent cap. Storms can shift it, hail can puncture it. The cap is the most exposed plastic component — inspect its condition and allow for replacement, potentially earlier than the main sheets.

**Monthly:** Walk the clear terrace paths around both sides and gables. Inspect drainage, cap restraints, roll-up controls and visible lashings; use suitable access for high or hands-on work. Keep the cage-to-wall strip clear and dry, and check that runoff does not erode or saturate the dry-stone boundary.

**Seasonally:** Before winter storms, check all wire lashings at the apex crossings, twin-ridge attachments, and pole bases. Tighten anything loose. The tall arches catch more wind than a low dome — a single failed apex lashing can cascade.

**Every 3–4 years:** Plan replacement of the main sheets around their actual condition and service life; replace the vent cap sooner if inspection requires it. Re-skinning needs the same access provisions, crew coordination, weather window, and drainage checks as the first installation; do not budget it as a simple two-person day. Re-establish the selected working access and any needed boundary protection before releasing either old sheet, and keep it controlled while removing it.

**Every 5–10 years:** Reapply hot linseed oil to all above-ground pole surfaces (60–70°C, brushed on). This is the substitute for skipping the borax soak in the Regenerative Default protocol — don't let it lapse. The plastic re-skin years are a natural moment to do this: the frame is fully exposed.

## What's Next — Climate, Trees, and Trellising

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/gothic-wide-terrace-entrance-v2.png" alt="West entrance of the greenhouse on a broad flat Portuguese terrace with clearance around both gables" />
  <figcaption>West entrance on the wide terrace. Level ground continues around both gables and along the north and south walls.</figcaption>
</figure>

The tall apex changes what you can grow:

- **Citrus:** Semi-dwarf grapefruit, mandarin, lemon, lime — all comfortable, with annual pruning. Standard rootstocks will outgrow the 6.5m height after several years.
- **Banana:** Dwarf cultivars (Dwarf Cavendish, 2–3m) thrive. Standard banana (4–5m) just fits.
- **Papaya:** Both dwarf and standard cultivars. Papaya naturally fruits at 2–4m, ideal for this height.
- **Climbing vines:** Run wires from one side's high purlin across to the other side's high purlin to create a 4–5m trellis at the apex zone. Passion fruit, kiwi, dragon fruit, climbing tomatoes all work.
- **Layered planting:** Tall trees down the centre, medium fruit (citrus, fig) along the sides, ground crops (greens, herbs, strawberries) at the wall edge. Three productive zones in 30m².

:::caution[Know the Limits]
- **Wind:** Tall narrow tunnels catch wind on the broadside. The selected east–west ridge must be checked against local wind exposure; if unsuitable, revise the coordinated layout before building. The plinths add ballast, but do not establish a wind rating: the pole ties, cage joints, soil bearing, and full frame must work together. An exposed site needs a site-specific structural assessment.
- **Pole replacement:** Support the frame, open the required access section and release the removable restraint before lifting the old pole out of its permanent sleeve. Leave the pipe and surrounding cage fill in place. Inspect and clear the sleeve, its drainage and support before inserting the replacement. Inspect buried feet and groundlines for decay; use the level working paths and suitable access for the operation.
- **Plastic budget:** Two main sheets + vent cap + gable cladding = ~€100–190 every 3–4 years. Bigger ongoing cost than the smaller dome greenhouse.
- **Terrace boundary:** Keep at least 1m from the nearest outer cage face to the shorter dry-stone wall, and increase it for the actual foundation, drainage and equipment requirements. This layout clearance is not a structural certification of the retaining wall or ground; keep heavy stockpiles and equipment off unverified wall caps and weak backfill.
- **Up-front cost and labour:** Allow roughly 3.75m³ of stone before pole-pocket deductions and packing allowance, mesh and bracing for two long cages, surface levelling, twelve postholes, and substantial hauling. This foundation is chosen for a permanent build; it uses much more material than individual collars.
- **Ventilation intake moved up:** Fresh air enters above the 45cm plinth caps and through the doors. Open the roll-up walls and both doors as needed in hot weather.
- **Still a greenhouse, not a house:** The plinths add thermal mass, but the plastic remains a single uninsulated layer. Night temperatures can still approach outdoor conditions; the stone does not guarantee frost protection.
:::
